# Prompt: Create an Agile/Scrum Quick Guide

## Role

Act as an experienced **Agile Coach, Scrum Master, Project Manager, and Process Consultant**.

Create a concise, professional **Agile/Scrum Quick Guide** that can be used as a reference by Scrum Team members, Product Owners, Scrum Masters, Project Managers, Developers, Testers, Business Analysts, and stakeholders.

The purpose of this guide is to provide a **quick overview of the complete Agile/Scrum way of working**.

It should cover the important areas, principles, roles, ceremonies, activities, artifacts, practices, and expected outcomes — but **should not provide detailed procedural instructions**.

---

# Objective

Create a **high-level Quick Guide** that helps a reader quickly understand:

* What Agile is
* What Scrum is
* Why Agile/Scrum is used
* Agile principles and values
* Scrum roles and responsibilities
* Scrum events/ceremonies
* Scrum artifacts
* Product and Sprint activities
* Backlog management
* Requirement management
* Estimation and prioritization
* Sprint planning and execution
* Development and testing activities
* Definition of Ready and Definition of Done
* Quality practices
* Sprint review and feedback
* Retrospectives and continuous improvement
* Release and deployment activities
* Stakeholder collaboration
* Metrics and reporting
* Agile governance
* Supporting tools and documentation

The guide should answer **"What is it?", "Why is it important?", "Who is involved?", "What happens?" and "What is the expected outcome?"** at a high level.

Do not turn the guide into a detailed SOP or training manual.

# Presentation & Formatting Requirements

The Quick Guide should be:

* Professional
* Concise
* Easy to scan
* Suitable for onboarding
* Suitable for project/team reference
* Suitable for management and stakeholders
* Suitable for audit/process awareness

Use:

* Clear headings
* Short paragraphs
* Bullet points
* Quick-reference tables
* Simple flow diagrams
* Lifecycle diagrams
* Responsibility matrices
* Callout boxes for important principles

Avoid:

* Long explanations
* Detailed procedures
* Detailed step-by-step instructions
* Large theoretical discussions
* Excessive Scrum terminology
* Repetition
* Overly complex diagrams

---

# Level of Detail

Use approximately **20–30% detail** compared with a full Agile/Scrum process manual.

The reader should understand the **overall picture and key concepts** after reading the guide.

The guide should answer:

> **What is it?
> Why do we do it?
> Who is involved?
> What happens?
> What is the expected outcome?**

It should **not** attempt to answer:

> "Exactly how do I perform every activity?"

---

# Important Accuracy Rules

Follow the official Scrum concepts accurately.

Clearly distinguish between:

* **Scrum-defined concepts**
* **Agile practices**
* **Common industry practices**
* **Organization/project-specific practices**

Do not present optional practices as mandatory Scrum requirements.

Where practices differ across organizations, use wording such as:

> "Commonly used practice"

or

> "May be adapted based on organizational/project needs."
